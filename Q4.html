The Hidden Cost of Age

While younger Australians are also frequently involved in road crashes, this heatmap reveals a stark contrast in the severity of those injuries. The visual gradient is undeniable: as you move down the age groups, the colour intensifies. Younger cohorts consistently require shorter hospital stays (averaging 2–4 days), whereas adults aged 65–74 require significantly longer care. The situation is most critical for those aged 75 and over, whose average hospital stay frequently exceeds 10 days, averaging 16.8 days in 2021. This reveals the “hidden severity” of road trauma: older adults may not make up the largest volume of crashes, but when they are hospitalized, the human cost and the strain on our healthcare system are disproportionately massive.

At a Glance

  • Younger Adults (17–25): Average stay of ~4 days.
  • Older Adults (75+): Average stay climbs to 16.8 days by 2021.
  • The Takeaway: In 2021, older adults require more than four times the hospital recovery time compared to young adults (16.8 days vs. 4.0 days).

Source: Australian Government, Department of Infrastructure, Transport, Regional Development, Communications and the Arts (2023).
